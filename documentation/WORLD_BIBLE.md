# WORLD BIBLE — Europe, winter 1964 (fictional alternative history)

Status: AUTHORED 2026-11-04; the divergence/armistice details remain OPEN for the user's historical review
(preproduction decision D10). Nothing here claims to describe real history.

## Timeline (fictional after 1941)
| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1939–41 | War begins as in our history. |
| 1942–49 | The eastern war bogs down into an attritional front; both economies collapse in stages; famine and partisan war behind the lines. |
| 1950–53 | Exhaustion; neutral mediation (Sweden, Switzerland, Finland); **Armistice of Veltrava** (March 1953) freezes the *Line*, a fortified demarcation through Eastern Europe. No peace treaty. |
| 1953–60 | Uneven reconstruction. Germany rebuilds Berlin as a monumental capital; the fictional **Große Halle** is completed in 1961 (in real history it was never built). The USSR rebuilds heavy industry; many western-Soviet towns stay half-ruined. |
| 1963 | **Corridor Accord** signed in Veltrava: three rail corridors across the Line, mixed commissions, neutral observers. |
| 1964 | First winter of full Accord traffic. The game begins in January. |

## Political reality
* **German Reich (1964):** one-party dictatorship; racial laws, surveillance, forced labour legacy, censorship;
  crimes of the 1940s are officially denied and privately known. Order and "recovery" are the propaganda themes.
  Berlin's government district is monumental; ordinary districts have housing shortages and evictions for
  "planning measures".
* **Soviet Union (1964):** one-party state; State Security with informant networks, labour camps, deportations of
  "unreliable" groups, censorship. Propaganda: peace, reconstruction, vigilance against the Reich. Communities
  survive through cooperation, barter, shared kitchens and humour.
* **Neutral zone:** the **Free City of Veltrava** on the Line (fictional), run by an international commission;
  spies, exiles, smugglers, journalists, interpreters.

## Symbols and language policy (binding, see ART_DIRECTION §4 and TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE §12.4)
* Real regime insignia (swastika banners and flags, the state eagle on stamps and plaques; hammer & sickle, red
  star) are part of the in-world iconography of both states when the setting "Historische Hoheitszeichen" is on
  (default); off → the fictional **Reichssiegel** (white pillar/obelisk in a black disc) or a plain eagle, and a
  Soviet gear-and-wheat emblem. No SS runes, no real party slogans. Never usable as rewards, collectibles or
  player decoration; uniforms grey, unit insignia generic.
* Soviet imagery: stars, gears, red banners, fictional slogans of reconstruction (e.g. "ВОССТАНОВИМ ВМЕСТЕ").
* Text in the world is German, Russian or both; UI German first (localisation-ready).
* Opening disclaimer: fictional alternative history; the game condemns the crimes of both regimes and does not
  equate them.

## Everyday material culture (environment storytelling checklist)
Ration and housing notices, queues, stovepipes through windows, mismatched roof sheets, laundry lines, tea and
kvass stalls, trams with handwritten route boards, radios with propaganda and music, sealed doors with official
paper strips, typewriters, card indexes, rubber stamps, lead seals, ledgers, carbon copies, blueprints (blue-line
diazo), samovars, enamel signs, Trabant-like and GAZ-like vehicles (original designs), steam and early diesel
locomotives.
