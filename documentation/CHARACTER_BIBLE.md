## Alexei Morozov — 29, Soviet railway engineer (playable)
* **Look:** shapka with ear-flaps, padded dark-green coat, carefully repaired boots, technical satchel; economical
  gestures; reads clearly as a small silhouette.
* **Life:** checks wagons and switch logs on the Karsk junction; helps a neighbour family with coal although he
  has little; mother Vera in a communal flat; childhood friend Pyotr defected in 1957 (unknown to the state as a
  friendship — a danger later).
* **Surface / inner conflict:** dutiful, distrusts German officials, believes regulations are the last reliable
  order. His work keeps the line running — and may serve a coercive registry. *Can you repair a system without
  sharing its consequences?*
* **Arc:** discover facts → see consequences for neighbours → stop equating strangers with states → take
  responsibility for his own silence. He never learns to trust repressive structures.
* **Voice:** short questions, precise observations, dry humour, never cynical about victims.
* **Gameplay identity:** physical clues, machinery and routing, repair, community help, survival situations.

## Konrad Adler — 31, Berlin architect / technical draftsman (playable)
* **Look:** fedora, long dark-blue overcoat, scarf, leather folder and drafting tube; upright posture that loosens
  as the story progresses.
* **Life:** works on reconstruction drawings in the Generalbauamt archive; lives in a sublet room; sister Hanna,
  a teacher, lives in a tenement inside the planned clearance zone; mentor Prof. Albrecht Reinholt.
* **Surface / inner conflict:** believes technical work is politically neutral; has lived inside the propaganda
  system and seldom questioned it. His precise drawings make administration efficient and legitimise coercion.
* **Arc:** contradiction between plan and reality → naming responsibility → seeing people instead of the staged
  state → concrete help and protecting evidence. No exoneration fantasy for the regime.
* **Voice:** longer, exact sentences; euphemistic at first, increasingly direct.
* **Gameplay identity:** floor plans and overlays, archives, bureaucracy, social stealth, spatial clues.

## Writing rules
Characters may be wrong and learn; perpetrator ideologies are never presented as legitimate positions. Dialogue
shows specific people and power relations instead of equating Nazi and Soviet crimes. Nobody is a mouthpiece.
