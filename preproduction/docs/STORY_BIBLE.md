# Story bible v0.1 — draft, subject to revision

## Logline
In a divided Europe where a devastating war ended without a victor, a Soviet railway engineer and a German architect independently uncover how a new cross-border reconstruction program could become an instrument of mass political control. Their investigation forces each to question the stories they inherited about the other country.

## Themes
Memory versus official history; the distinction between civilians and states; complicity, fear, responsibility, trust earned through action, the costs of reconstruction.

## Historical framing
This is speculative history, not an assertion about real 1964 Europe. The Nazi regime is portrayed as an oppressive criminal dictatorship, including its racial persecution; the story must not glamorize its symbols or ideology. Soviet repression and censorship are treated critically and specifically, not as a false equivalence.

## Protagonists
### Alexei Morozov (29)
A Soviet railway engineer responsible for restoring a damaged freight corridor. Values reliability and helping neighbors; distrusts officials but assumes Germans share their government's hostility. Arc: learns to distinguish people from the institutions governing them while confronting his own silence.

### Matthias Adler (31)
A German technical draftsman assigned to Berlin reconstruction records. Trained to keep his head down and believe official accounts. Arc: discovers his own role in legitimizing a repressive system and chooses whether to protect his career or other people.

## Supporting cast (original)
- **Irina Belova**, depot dispatcher: Alexei's practical ally; protects her coworkers, skeptical of heroic gestures.
- **Lev Sokolov**, archival clerk: knows records were altered; afraid of the consequences of disclosure.
- **Marta Vogel**, Berlin tram operator: knows the city through everyday routes and forced relocations.
- **Emil Hartmann**, municipal archivist: can provide access to blueprints but is compromised by his employment.
- **Elena Weiss**, bilingual conference interpreter: helps the protagonists understand not only words but competing assumptions; retains her own agenda.

## Central mystery
The officially humanitarian **Corridor Accord** is a reconstruction network linking rail logistics, registry systems and border infrastructure. Its public value is real; a covert administrative layer enables surveillance, forced relocation and political pressure. Different institutions pursue conflicting agendas. Avoid implausible single-mastermind explanations.

## Narrative rule
Neither protagonist can learn everything alone. Repeated objects, places and records allow the player to understand more than either character at a given moment.

## Endings (design candidates)
1. **Public Record:** evidence reaches independent witnesses; affected communities are protected in some regions, but the governments remain powerful.
2. **Quiet Safeguards:** key mechanisms are disabled and civilians helped, while most records remain secret.
3. **Broken Trust:** the evidence is fragmented, relations deteriorate, and personal costs rise.
The outcomes depend on earlier flags and relationships, not one final menu choice.
