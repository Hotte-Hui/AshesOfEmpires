### Character arcs
- **Alexei:** duty to keep civilian rail transport functioning vs silence about coercive diversion; learns that reporting to authority alone does not settle responsibility.
- **Matthias:** moves from professional neutrality to acknowledging that drawings can facilitate removal and control. Neither an unearned instant reformer nor an uncritical agent.
- **Irina:** a full colleague with agency; she may refuse to cooperate after a breach of confidence.
- **Emil:** imperfect archivist, constrained by prior compromises; assistance never absolves complicity.
- **Marta/Elena/Lev:** not yet scripted as slice NPCs. Reserve scenes in future chapter planning; do not fabricate full gameplay content for them.

### Scene-to-scene causal thread
1. Alexei finds freight ledger + timetable contradictions; the symbols on a fictitious idle teaching board gate careful observation rather than real rail operations.
2. A deliberate **match cut** carries the viewer (but **not** the character's knowledge) to Matthias's archived plan.
3. Matthias overlays drawings, finds a hidden corridor on the record. Emil clarifies C-17 refers to a contract series.
4. Berlin's fictional Große Halle appears framed by people facing eviction and ordinary bureaucracy; the scene is critical, not celebratory.
5. Demo endpoint emphasizes two plausible evidence trails and sets up their first in-person contact at a later conference, **not in the current slice**.

### Narrative acceptance checks
- Every choice is acknowledged in the journal and has at least one planned later callback; avoid a single cosmetic final choice.
- No hero can solve the transnational conspiracy by reading one letter. Each finds **part** of the story.
- Architecture must not misrepresent the actual historical Volkshalle as constructed; fictional finished landmark exists **only** in the alternate timeline.
- Historical Nazi persecution and Soviet repression are not interchangeable aesthetic motifs. Seek historical sensitivity review before any public release.
